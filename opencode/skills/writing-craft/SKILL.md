# Skill: writing-craft

## When to Load

Load alongside any section skill (intro, methods, results, discussion, lit-review-section) when drafting or revising. Load alone for structural planning and outlining.

## Paper Types

### Empirical Papers

- IMRaD / martini glass structure
- Drafting order: Methods → Results → Discussion → Intro → Abstract → Title
  (figures and analysis clarify what the story is before the story is told)
- Focus on what was found and what it means for theory/practice
- Contribution: what the data show that wasn't known before

### Theoretical Papers

- Problem → Approach → Contribution → Implications
- Structure is argument-driven, not data-driven
- Focus on conceptual reorganization: what new lens does this paper offer?
- Contribution: what the field should think differently after reading

### Meta-Research / Methods Papers

- Problem with current practice → Proposed solution → Evidence it works
- Often include simulations, re-analyses, or systematic reviews

## Contribution Framing

See `mcguire-heuristics`, "For Writer" section for the full list.

Frame the contribution as a generative act, not just a topic.
Name what kind of intellectual move the paper makes:

- Overturning an assumption: "Contrary to the prevailing view that..."
- Reversing causal direction: "Where prior work assumed A → B, we test B → A"
- Adjudicating among explanations: "We test N alternative accounts and show..."
- Resolving a conflict: "Prior studies disagree; we identify the moderator that explains the discrepancy"
- Transferring a mechanism by analogy: "We apply [mechanism] from [domain A] to [domain B]"
- Integrating complementary lines: "We unite three literatures under a single framework"
- Decomposing complexity: "The apparently complex function decomposes into N simpler processes"

## Perspectivism

Knowledge is inherently perspectival. Good writing reflects this.

- Present multiple theoretical perspectives on the topic, not just the dominant framework.
  The introduction should surface competing views, not just set up a single-position straw man.
- Frame gaps as "what existing perspectives miss" not just "what hasn't been studied."
  A gap in the literature is often a gap in PERSPECTIVE — a lens that hasn't been applied.
- In discussions, interpret findings through multiple theoretical lenses.
  If a finding is consistent with several perspectives, say so — don't pick one winner.
- Avoid single-framework absolutism. No single theory captures all of reality.
  The best papers show what their perspective reveals AND what it misses.
- Frame limitations as "what this perspective doesn't capture"
  rather than only methodological weaknesses. Intellectual humility is a strength.

## Paragraph and Revision Craft

For paragraph structure (hamburger, old-to-new flow, end-weight,
concrete words) and revision technique (reverse outlining,
nurture-before-prune), follow STYLE.md §4 and §5, and the

## Remember

1. Frame the contribution as a generative act — name the intellectual move.
2. Multiple perspectives are a virtue, not a weakness.
