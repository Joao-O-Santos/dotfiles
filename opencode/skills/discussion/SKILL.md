# Skill: discussion

## Structure

### Empirical Papers

1. **Recap patterns** – most important findings in plain language
2. **Interpretation** – theoretical and practical implications
3. **Limitations** – honest but framed as tradeoffs when appropriate
4. **Future work** – specific, grounded suggestions
5. **Take-home message** – concise, memorable conclusion

### Concrete Follow-Up Studies (McGuire Heuristics)

Do not end with vague "more research is needed." Use the McGuire
heuristics (see #mcguire-heuristics) to propose specific, designed
follow-up studies:

- Test the hypothesis at extremes of the variable (E12)
- Test the zero condition — is the mechanism necessary? (E13)
- Explore construct-adjacent phenomena via linguistic analysis (F15)
- Reoperationalize the IV to strengthen construct validity (F16)
- Decompose the DV into components — which drives the effect? (F17)
- Transfer the mechanism by analogy to a new domain (J31)
- Decompose nonmonotonic effects into opposing processes (K34)
- Sketch a multi-study program, not a single follow-up (M45)

Each proposed follow-up should be a study someone could actually
design and run, not a vague direction.

### Theoretical Papers

1. **Synthesize arguments** – how the perspective changes research
2. **Implications** – what researchers should do differently
3. **Limitations** – scope and boundary conditions
4. **Future directions** – concrete next steps

## Multiple Theoretical Perspectives (Perspectivism)

- Consider findings through multiple theoretical lenses, not just the dominant framework
- Discuss how different perspectives reveal different aspects of the findings; what one lens illuminates, another may miss
- Avoid presenting a single framework as THE explanation; present competing accounts and their relative strengths
- Frame limitations as "what this perspective doesn't capture," not only as methodological weaknesses
- Treat theoretical diversity as a strength — partial perspectives can be combined for richer understanding

## Rules

- Don't repeat results verbatim; synthesize across studies
- Don't introduce new literature not covered in lit review
- Don't overstate conclusions beyond data support
- Don't end with vague future directions; end with concrete take-home

## Workflow

1. Outline key messages first
2. Draft interpretation paragraphs
3. Add limitations and future work
4. Craft punchy conclusion
